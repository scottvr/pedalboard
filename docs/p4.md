# ESP32-P4: the project after the kit

Notes from the 2026-10-12 evening conversation, written down 2026-10-13. Nothing here is
started; it is the shape of the next project and the facts gathered so far. The LAFVIN kit
work in `BACKLOG.md` comes first and does not depend on any of it.

## The idea

NAM on an ESP32-P4. The odds of the pedal running on one are good, and of running better than
on the S3 decent, because the chip removes the two walls the S3 port keeps hitting:

- **Cores.** Two RISC-V HP cores at 400 MHz with single-precision FPUs and a 128-bit SIMD
  extension that Espressif's DSP library already uses on the P4. The S3 does the same work at
  240 MHz on two LX7 cores at about 87% of the 1,333 µs block. The LP core (40 MHz) is for
  sleep-time housekeeping: it could own the footswitch and LEDs, not a sample of audio.
- **Memory.** About 768 KB of internal SRAM with no IRAM-versus-DRAM trade, plus PSRAM on a
  faster bus (32 MB on the boards below). Most of the pedal's memory document, the bank
  scheme, the IRAM reclaims and the arena lending exist because the S3 has 512 KB split
  awkwardly; on the P4 that largely dissolves. PSRAM is still slower than internal SRAM, so
  the hot path still wants to live in L2MEM.
- **USB.** High-Speed host. The pedal's whole UAC story is shaped by the S3 being Full Speed
  only; on the P4 the Scarlett and MOTU class of interfaces come into range and UAC2 at 24-bit
  stereo stops being a capacity exercise.
- **Flash.** 16 to 32 MB on the boards below, so the OTA slots stop being a budget line (the
  current LAFVIN image fills about 60% of a 4 MB slot).

The real cost is the NAM kernels. The pedal's hot path is hand-written Xtensa assembly and
none of it assembles for RISC-V: about 2,000 lines across five files in `coyopedal/src`
(`audio/nam/s3_native_fused_layer8.S` 1,311, `audio/nam/s3_native_head16.S` 224,
`audio/nam/s3_quantize8_q24.S` 108, `audio/cabinet_fft64.S` 227,
`native/audio/s3_pcm_pack.S` 146), plus a few lines of inline Xtensa in `audio/effects.cpp`
(`rsr.ccount`, `recip0.s`, `round.s`) and the S3-shaped C++ around the kernels in
`audio/nam/nam_a2_full_s3_native.cpp`. Everything above the kernels (effects graph, storage,
UI, maintenance) is C++ and TSX and moves.

**Correction (2026-11-14):** an earlier version of this note said the kernels would be
rewritten "against the P4's vector unit". The P4 does not implement the standard RISC-V
vector extension (RVV). Its SIMD is Espressif's PIE, the same family as the S3's: 128-bit Q
registers and a QACC accumulator, with `esp.` mnemonics where the S3 has `ee.`. So the port
is a translation of the S3 kernels (same int16 MAC-into-QACC design), not a new design.
What changes, and what to verify before relying on it:

- **The ISA is barely documented.** As of April 2026 the P4 TRM's PIE chapter was still
  empty; community instruction lists exist and at least one is unreliable. Ground truth is
  what Espressif's toolchain assembles and what esp-dsp's P4 assembly uses. Confirm every
  mnemonic there, never from a forum list.
- **QACC is wider.** Espressif's PIE introduction gives the P4's QACC_H and QACC_L as 256
  bits each (the S3's are narrower). The S3 kernel's "exact low-32 reconstruction" of QACC,
  whose read order was verified on silicon, does not carry over; that part is re-derived.
- **Variants may be missing.** An open question on Espressif's forum asks whether the S3's
  `.qup` variants of `vmulas` exist on the P4. Check each instruction the S3 kernels use
  (`ee.vld.128.ip`, `ee.vmulas.s16.qacc`, `ee.vsmulas.s16.qacc.ld.incp`, `ee.vzip`/`vunzip`,
  `ee.srcmb.s16.qacc`, `ee.ldf/stf.64.ip` among them) for a P4 equivalent before planning.
- **ABI.** RISC-V's calling convention replaces the Xtensa windowed one: every entry, exit
  and stack frame is redone.
- **Caches.** The P4 runs through L1/L2 caches; the S3 kernels assume flat SRAM timing.
  Expect the first port to miss its cycle estimate until data layout suits the cache.

Two things make it tractable. The S3 kernels have an instruction-level **host twin** in
`nam_a2_full_s3_native.cpp` (search "host twin"): a C++ model of the kernels that runs on the
host, which is the correctness oracle for each translated kernel. And at 400 MHz with an
FPU, a **scalar** NAM may be fast enough for a first boot, so audio can work before any
assembly exists and give the vector port a number to beat.

The P4 has no radio. Its boards pair it with an ESP32-C6 over SDIO and Wi-Fi goes through
Espressif's remote-Wi-Fi layer (`esp_wifi_remote`), so the maintenance link is a different
code path from today's on-chip driver: the one place beyond the kernels where the pedal's
maintenance service needs real changes.

## Where Geastack stands on the P4

- The targets package carries three P4 board directories: `esp32-p4-m5stack-tab5` and
  `esp32-p4-waveshare-touch-lcd-7` (ILI9881 panels over MIPI-DSI, GT911 touch) and
  `esp32-p4-waveshare-touch-lcd-3.5` (ST7796). The CLI's board catalog lists them and flashes
  P4 over USB Serial/JTAG at 921600.
- The maintainer said on 2026-10-12 that he owns no P4 hardware. So those directories were
  written from vendor board support packages and have never run: the DSI display pipeline and
  the remote Wi-Fi layer are unverified assumptions in that corner of the tree. "Nobody has
  tried" is the honest status, and the first boot of any P4 board here is also Gea's first
  P4 run.
- The composer has one base, the S3. A new P4 board today is a hand-written target directory
  like those three, not a JSON definition.
- The maintainer also said: "[the P4] is a different architecture not currently supported by
  coyopedal, and I have made the decision not to open source it myself." Read as a statement
  about his own work (he has P4 work and is keeping it closed, which the Gea embedded
  targets' commercial-licence path exists for), not as a line against ours: coyopedal and the
  targets are GPLv3 and a port in the open is allowed. Practically it means an open P4 port
  would probably not be merged upstream. Before starting, ask him the courtesy question,
  about his plans rather than ours: is an open P4 port something he would rather not see, or
  just something he would not merge. The umbrella repo is the home for a port he does not
  take.

## Sequence, when the kit is done

1. First light of a Gea P4 target on a board on hand (blink, then the C6 link). Gea's first
   P4 run.
2. Headless pedal boot on the POE-ETH board with the ES8311 path, kernels stubbed or scalar.
   Both boards on hand are panel-less, so this is the LAFVIN headless-first route again, with
   a hand-written P4 target directory (the composer has no P4 base). Here the ES8311 stops
   being the speaker: it is the first codec-as-interface test, mic in and speaker out, with
   the pedal's audio device abstraction gaining a codec device beside the USB host
   (`BACKLOG.md`, "Analog I/O as the pedal's interface").
3. NAM kernels on the P4's PIE (translated from the S3 kernels, checked against the host
   twin), measured against the S3's 87%. The headroom the
   analog path and the stretch goals assume is this number; nothing is measured yet.
4. Maintenance over Ethernet first (one PoE cable), `esp_wifi_remote` second.
5. Analog I/O breakout on the 40-pin header: an ADC and DAC with TRS jacks and an analog front
   end for the guitar (chip choice open, see the backlog item), a Gea binding for the codec,
   and the pedal's TRS in and out. Then the 5-DSI-TOUCH-A for display and touch.

## Running the P4 track in a separate Claude Code session

Written 2026-11-14 for a second session on another machine, so the kit work here and the
P4 port can run side by side. A new session there starts with no memory of this one: the
per-machine memory under `~/.claude/projects/` does not travel, so what it needs is here.

**Read first, in order:** `CLAUDE.md`, this file, `docs/branching.md` (the branch model),
`docs/build-environment.md`, then `coyopedal/AGENTS.md`. The S3 memory story the P4 lets you
simplify is in the pedal repo's own docs; `BACKLOG.md` is the kit's sprint and is not this
track's to-do list.

**Before any code:** the maintainer has closed P4 work of his own (see "Where Geastack
stands" above). The user asks him the courtesy question first; the session does not
contact upstream or open PRs on this track.

**Setup on the other machine:**

1. `gh repo clone scottvr/pedalboard`, then clone `coyopedal`, `cli`, `targets`, `core` into
   it as `CLAUDE.md` describes. `docs/dnd/` is gitignored and does not come along; never
   recreate or distribute its contents.
2. Paths in `CLAUDE.md` assume `/Volumes/idf6`. On another machine, write the local paths
   into a `CLAUDE.local.md` (gitignored here) rather than editing
   `CLAUDE.md`.
3. ESP-IDF 6.0.2 with the P4 toolchain: `./install.sh esp32p4` (riscv32-esp-elf).
4. Read each board's silicon revision first (`esptool --chip esp32p4 chip-id`). Pre-v3
   silicon needs IDF's Kconfig option for older revisions; confirm the option name in IDF
   6.0.2's `esp32p4` Kconfig, don't guess it.
5. The P4 boards (POE-ETH first) plug into that machine.

**Branches** (per `docs/branching.md`): in `coyopedal`, a topic branch `p4-port` off the
pedal's upstream main, not off `lavfin-kit-notes`. In the forks, one topic branch per change
(`p4-poe-eth-target`, ...) and one integration branch `pedal-p4`. Never commit to a fork's
`main`; never push unless the user asks.

**Sharing the workspace repo between two sessions.** This session owns `BACKLOG.md`,
`README.md` and every doc but the P4 ones. The P4 session owns this file and any new
`docs/p4-*.md`. `git pull --rebase` before every commit and keep commits small, so the two
machines rarely conflict. The user decides when the workspace repo is pushed.

**Order of work.** Follow "Sequence" above:

1. Gea's first P4 boot.
2. Headless pedal with scalar kernels.
3. The PIE port.

Hardware-free work that can start first:
- a survey of P4 PIE mnemonics from esp-dsp's P4 sources and the toolchain's assembler, mapped one to one against the S3 instructions the kernels use, written to `docs/p4-pie.md`;
- building the host twin and the scalar path for the host.

Don't flash a board unless the user asks, as everywhere in this workspace.

**Model and effort.** Use Claude Fable 5.2 (released 2026-11-09) as the session's model.
On Anthropic's published Humanity's Last Exam (with tools) chart, Fable 5.2 at low effort
(68.4%, about $0.20 a task) beats every earlier model at any effort. Its curve is flat from
low to medium, and max gains under a point over xhigh at about three times the cost. HLE is
not embedded work, so treat this as a rough guide:
- low or medium for the boot, read the log, change one thing loop and for bookkeeping;
- high or xhigh for translating the kernel instructions and re-deriving the QACC readout;
- max is rarely worth it.

**On orchestration.** Most of this is sequential and hardware-bound: boot a board, read a
log, change one thing. A single session in plan mode does that better than a fan-out of
agents. The places parallel agents do pay are bounded and checkable: the mnemonic survey,
and later translating kernels file by file, where each file has the host twin as its test.

### Developing on a cloud VM

Most of the track before the PIE kernels meet silicon needs no board, so the session can
live on a Linux VM. EC2 suits it; Colab does not: its runtimes are ephemeral and have no
persistent shell, which Claude Code needs. Any instance type works: x86_64, or Graviton
(IDF ships linux-arm64 tools). Suggested size: about 8 vCPUs and 16 GB of RAM (a clean
pedal build is about forty minutes on the Intel Mac here), with 60 GB or more of EBS (IDF,
its tools, node_modules and one build directory per target).

What runs on the VM:
- Every build: IDF 6.0.2 with `./install.sh esp32s3,esp32p4`, Node, and the pedal's
  `npm install`.
- The host tests. `npm test` (`coyopedal/scripts/test.sh`) builds the instruction-level host
  twin with plain `c++` and runs it against the real models in `assets/models/`. The script
  already handles non-Darwin hosts.
- The PIE mnemonic survey, with the `riscv32-esp-elf` assembler and objdump from IDF's
  tools.

Don't count on an emulator. Espressif's QEMU fork covered the ESP32, C3 and S3 when this was
written, not the P4. Check before relying on one; otherwise kernels meet real silicon only
on a board.

**Getting images to a board.** Build on the VM, then flash on the Mac with the boards, by
one of two routes:

1. **Copy and flash.** `scp` the `bootloader.bin`, `partition-table.bin`,
   `ota_data_initial.bin` and app `.bin` to the Mac and flash with esptool, as
   `docs/build-environment.md` does for the S3, with P4 options. Simple, and the user stays
   in the loop for every flash.
2. **Remote serial.** Run esptool's `esp_rfc2217_server` on the Mac against the board's
   port, reachable from the VM only through an SSH reverse tunnel
   (`ssh -R 4000:localhost:4000 vm`). The VM then flashes and monitors with
   `--port rfc2217://localhost:4000`. Never expose the RFC2217 port to the network: it has
   no authentication. It also lets the VM flash on its own, so the "don't flash unless asked"
   rule matters more here, not less.

**Credentials on the VM.** Give `gh` a fine-grained token scoped to the scottvr forks and
the workspace repo, not the user's full login, and no AWS keys beyond what the instance
needs. `docs/dnd/` does not exist there and must not be copied over.
